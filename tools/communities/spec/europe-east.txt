# Region EEU: population composition on 1925-01-01, as shares of each state.
# Modelling choices for this region:
# - Jews are counted by RELIGION (not declared nationality): ashkenazi in Central/Eastern Europe and
#   the Baltics, sephardi in the Balkans, Greece, Turkey and the Dodecanese (Corfu and Ioannina
#   Romaniotes folded into sephardi).
# - Religion "none"/"without confession" (Czech lands 1921) is folded into the tradition of origin
#   (catholic). Czechoslovak Hussite Church (1920) counted with protestant.
# - Unitarians (Transylvania) and Reformed/Calvinists counted as protestant. Old Believers and
#   Lipovans as orthodox. Mariavites as catholic.
# - Anatolian Alevis (Turkish and Kurdish) and Albanian Bektashis mapped to shia; the 1927 Turkish
#   and 1923 Albanian counts had no sect question, so those splits are estimates (flag M).
# - Yugoslavia 1921 counted "Serbian or Croatian" as one mother tongue: speakers are split by
#   religion (orthodox = serb, catholic = croat incl. Bunjevci/Sokci, muslim = bosniak).
#   Slavic speakers of Vardar Macedonia (Juzna Srbija) and the Slavophones of Greek Macedonia use the
#   key `macedonian`. Montenegrins are under `serb` (the census did not separate them).
# - Rusyns of Subcarpathia, eastern Slovakia, Vojvodina and Maramures use `rusyn`; Lemkos in the
#   Polish census ("ruska" nationality) stay under `ukrainian`.
# - Pomaks (Bulgarian-speaking Muslims) = bulgarian/sunni, also in Greece and Turkey.
# - Kashubians (Pomorze, Danzig) folded into polish; Arvanites (Orthodox Albanian speakers of Attica,
#   Boeotia, Epirus) folded into greek, as the 1928 census mostly recorded them.
# - Geometry: states follow modern units (states.ron), so 1921 voivodeship/land figures were blended
#   where the modern unit differs (noted per line).
